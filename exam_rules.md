# Exam rules

## Registration

Registration for each exam session is **mandatory**.

:information_source: Students from UniTS: please register on Esse3.

:information_source: Students from SISSA: please register by sending the instructor an email.

**Failure to register will result in exclusion from the exam.**

Students must present an **ID card or passport** for identification.

---

## Content

The exam covers all topics and material listed in the [course syllabus](syllabus.md) and in this repository, including lecture notes, recordings, examples, exercises and solutions, and referenced resources.

---

## Format

- Exams will be held **in-person**.
- Students are required to bring **their own laptop**.
- Students with **special needs** should contact the instructor well before the exam session.
- The **preferred platform** for submitting the exam and receiving the evaluation is **Google Classroom**. If, for any reason, you prefer not to create or use a Google account, please let the instructor know so that an alternative arrangement can be made.

### Structure

The main exam consists of two parts:

- **Part 1**: 10 open-ended questions will test theoretical understanding and application of concepts.
- **Part 2**: 1 programming exercise will assess practical skills in coding and problem-solving.

After the main exam, either the student or the instructor may request an **optional oral exam**.

The optional [course project](project_rules.md) is separate from the main exam. Its presentation and individual verification **do not constitute the optional oral exam** described below.

### Time allocation

- The exam lasts **three hours**: **one hour for Part 1** and **two hours for Part 2**, with a short break in between.
- Submissions after the deadline will not be accepted.

### Questions and technical issues

If you have questions or experience technical issues during the exam, please inform the exam invigilator immediately.

---

## Academic integrity

The common definitions and procedures in the [academic integrity rules](academic_integrity.md) apply to the main exam. **Compliance is mandatory.**

**All answers and solution-specific code must be produced independently by the student during the examination.**

Communication or collaboration with anyone other than an exam invigilator is **prohibited** during both parts. Students may contact invigilators only about instructions or technical issues.

### Part 1: permitted resources

**Part 1 is a closed-book, closed-device assessment.**

Computers, mobile phones, electronic devices, textbooks, notes, online resources, AI tools, and all other external resources are **prohibited**.

### Part 2: permitted and prohibited resources

**Part 2 is an open-book assessment.**

Students may consult:
- textbooks and personal notes;
- course material and documentation;
- locally or publicly available technical documentation;
- third-party open-source libraries, unless excluded by the exercise instructions.

Permitted references should support reasoning and application, not reproduce a pre-existing solution. Substantial external material incorporated into the submission must be acknowledged where appropriate.

The following are **prohibited** during Part 2:
- generative AI assistants, code generators, AI agents, and AI-enabled chat or search tools;
- online forums, question-and-answer services, and social media;
- messaging, email, shared documents, or any other communication with another person;
- obtaining, requesting, or receiving a solution or solution-specific assistance from any external party;
- accessing another student's work or allowing another student to access one's own work.

If a tool - including text editors, IDEs, or search engines - contains an integrated AI feature, **that feature must remain disabled and must not be used**.

#### Part 2: screen recording

Students must make a **continuous recording of the full computer screen throughout Part 2**, with audio and webcam disabled.

The recording:

- remains in the sole possession of the student;
- must not be uploaded or submitted automatically;
- must be kept unchanged until the evaluation process is completed;
- may be requested by the instructor only in the presence of justified doubts concerning the regularity of the exam or the coherence between the submitted solution and the expected competences.

If requested, the student must provide the recording according to the instructions and deadline communicated by the instructor.

Unless supported by documented technical issues, failure to provide a requested recording may result in **annulment of the exam attempt** or a **mandatory oral verification**.

Any requested recording will be used only for evaluation, accessed only by the instructor, and deleted once the evaluation is final, in accordance with applicable data-protection requirements.

#### Part 2: recording requirements

Students may use any reliable screen-recording software, provided that the recording:
- covers the entire screen;
- is continuous for the full duration of Part 2;
- does not include audio or webcam;
- is not paused, stopped, edited, or otherwise modified before the evaluation is finalized.

The preferred cross-platform option is [OBS Studio](https://obsproject.com/kb/quick-start-guide). Reliable operating-system recording tools are also acceptable, including [Windows Xbox Game Bar](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/windows/use-a-screen-reader-to-record-your-screen-with-xbox-game-bar-5328cd25-9046-4472-8a14-c485f138802c), the [built-in macOS screen recorder](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT208721), [GNOME Screen Recorder](https://itsfoss.com/gnome-screen-recorder/), and [SimpleScreenRecorder](https://www.maartenbaert.be/simplescreenrecorder/).

Students are responsible for installing and testing the software, recording the exam, and retaining an unchanged backup until evaluation is complete. Follow the TODO checklist below.

## Submission guidelines

### Part 1: Open-ended questions

- Answers to open-ended questions must be handwritten on paper and delivered to the instructor.

### Part 2: Programming exercise

- Submit the exam through Google Classroom using valid personal information and email address.
- Submit the solution as a single `.zip` or `.tar.gz` archive named `part2_Surname_Name_ID.{zip,tar.gz}` (where `ID` is your student identification number). The archive must contain the source code, tests, documentation, examples, and configuration files needed to reproduce the results.
- Include a `README.md` containing a brief description of the code structure and instructions for configuring, building, and running the code.

:warning: **Do not include** generated files, executables, object files, compiled libraries, build directories, virtual environments, or large generated datasets.

:warning: **Maintain backups of your work to prevent data loss.**

## Grading

### Part 1: 10 open-ended questions (up to 1.2 points each)

The evaluation will consider:
- clarity of expression;
- depth of understanding of the theoretical concepts;
- application and analysis of knowledge.

### Part 2: Programming exercise (up to 15 points)

The evaluation will consider:
- syntactical and logical correctness of the code;
- clarity and organization of code;
- code testing and efficiency;
- proper use of programming constructs and data structures covered during the course;
- documentation and comments explaining design choices, build and execution instructions, logic, and flow;
- successful compilation and execution in accordance with the problem statement;
- **(Bonus)** appropriate use of constructs and data structures beyond the course content.

### (Optional) Course project (up to +5 points)

Students may complete an incremental course project according to the separate [project rules](project_rules.md).

It includes a final software submission, documentation, a group presentation, and individual verification of each student's understanding and contribution.

The project score ranges from **0 to 5 points** and is added to the main exam grade. Because part of the assessment is individual, members of the same group may receive different scores.

### (Optional) Oral exam (from -3 to +3 points)

During the oral exam, students may be asked to:
- discuss theoretical concepts;
- explain, analyze, or correct code;
- justify design and implementation choices;
- solve short problems related to the course content.

The evaluation will consider:
- clarity of expression;
- depth of understanding of the theoretical concepts;
- ability to apply and connect the concepts covered in the course.

The optional oral exam is distinct from the presentation and individual verification included in the optional course project.

The maximum score for the **main exam (Part 1 + Part 2) is 27 points**.

The final grade is the sum of the main exam score, the optional project score, and, where applicable, the signed oral-exam adjustment.

Honors may be granted in exceptional cases, provided the final grade exceeds 30.

## TODO checklist for the main exam

### Before the exam
- [ ] **Install and test** a screen recording tool.
- [ ] Verify that **audio and webcam are disabled**.
- [ ] Ensure at least **1 GB of free disk space**.
- [ ] Perform a **test recording of at least 5 minutes**.
- [ ] **Know** where the recording file is saved.

### At the start of the exam
- [ ] Ensure your computer is **charged or plugged into a power socket**.
- [ ] Ensure your **internet connection is stable**.
- [ ] **Start the screen recording** as soon as the exam starts.
- [ ] **Check** that the recording timer is running.

### During the exam
- [ ] **Keep track of time** and manage it effectively.
- [ ] **Read** each question carefully before answering.
- [ ] **Maintain backups of your work to prevent data loss.**
- [ ] Keep the recording **continuous**; do not pause or stop it.
- [ ] Immediately **report** any technical issue to the invigilator.

### At the end of the exam
- [ ] **Double-check** all submissions before the final upload.
- [ ] **Submit** the exam solution.
- [ ] Stop the recording **only after submission**.
- [ ] Store a **backup copy** of your recording to prevent loss.
- [ ] **Keep the recording unchanged** until the evaluation process is completed.
